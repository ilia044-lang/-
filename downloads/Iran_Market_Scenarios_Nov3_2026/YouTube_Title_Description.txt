If the U.S. & Israel Strike Iran Again: How Markets React and Recover

DESCRIPTION
If the U.S. and Israel launch another major strike on Iran, must stocks crash and oil surge? Historical reactions were very different. This episode compares five event dates across 28 market proxies and explains which real-world developments could turn a short-lived risk premium into a lasting economic shock.

This is CONDITIONAL SCENARIO ANALYSIS, not an announcement of a new strike, an attack-probability estimate or a prediction of prices.
Prepared November 3, 2026. Market snapshot: completed November 2 closes. News segment updated November 4: Reuters 10:53 UTC and AP 04:13 UTC headline metadata. Historical study remains dated November 3; prices are not live.

WHAT WE COMPARE
January 3, 2020: U.S. strike on Soleimani in Iraq, a contextual comparison rather than a strike inside Iran.
April 19, 2024: Israeli strike in Iran.
October 26, 2024: weekend Israeli strikes; first U.S. cash session October 28.
June 13, 2025: opening Israeli strikes.
June 22, 2025: U.S. nuclear-facility strikes; first U.S. cash session June 23. News and strike timing span different time zones.

RECOVERY DEFINITION
Baseline = last closing price before the first affected U.S. session. First regain = first daily close at or above that baseline. Counts are trading sessions AFTER the event session; zero means same-session regain/no initial closing loss. We also check two consecutive closes. ETF figures use price returns excluding distributions, not portfolio total returns. Bitcoin uses UTC calendar-day bars, which can straddle the first reports.

KEY FINDINGS
SPY first regained its baseline 4 sessions later in January 2020, 1 later in April 2024, and 6 later after June 13, 2025. It was already above baseline at the first close after the October 2024 and June 22, 2025 weekend events.
QQQ briefly regained its June 2025 baseline after 1 session, then fell below again; two consecutive closes were confirmed June 25.
The June events overlap. Five selected observations do not yield a reliable recovery forecast. Returns after 5 or 20 sessions include other news, earnings and policy changes.

AI stocks were getting hit. So how did equal weight stocks and small caps finish the week in green? The answer matters before next week's Fed decision, inflation data and giant technology earnings. In this review, we'll separate the index from the stocks inside it, unpack oil and the new cyber threat, and build a calm checklist for what comes next.

For the week ending October 23, SPY fell 0.43% and QQQ lost 0.76%. But the Dow gained 0.41%. The equal weight S&P fund, RSP, rose 1.14%, and small caps, represented by IWM, gained 1.96%. These are Friday to Friday price changes, not just Friday's session.

Why can those numbers disagree? A capitalization weighted index gives larger companies more influence. Equal weighting spreads influence more evenly. Imagine a classroom average where a few students count many times more than everyone else. If those students struggle, the average can fall even while many others improve. That is an illustration, not a calculation of this week's exact index contribution. The gap between SPY and RSP supports a rotation story, not proof that every smaller company was healthy.

Eight of the eleven major S&P sector ETFs finished the week higher. Energy gained about 1.6% and healthcare about 1.45%. Financials, utilities and real estate were also positive. Communication services fell 4.36%, while industrials lost 1.87%. This is why a single technology headline can give an incomplete picture. Sector funds are useful comparison tools, but they are not a count of every advancing stock.

There were severe losses in parts of the AI trade. CoreWeave fell about 11.65%, Meta lost 9.40% and Iren fell 7.73%. The semiconductor fund SOXX lost 3.08%. Yet Palantir rose 5.76%. So the honest description is selective damage and uneven leadership, not that every AI company collapsed. These examples illustrate the dispersion; they are not the entire sector.

The weekly return also hides the path. QQQ recovered to $725.20 on Wednesday, then fell to $704.47 on Thursday. That was a drop of about 2.86% in one session. Friday was almost unchanged for QQQ. Reuters reported that Meta's results reignited concern about AI spending. The sharp technology shock was concentrated late in the week, after an earlier recovery.

The ten year Treasury yield finished near 4.99%, about eleven basis points below the previous Friday. Lower yields can support valuations, but falling yields can also reflect concern about growth or demand for safer assets. Meanwhile, rising oil can create inflation pressure. Those forces can pull in opposite directions. This week's bond move is another reason not to describe the market with a single risk on or risk off label.

Here is another apparent contradiction. VIX ended at 19.45, below the previous Friday's 19.99. But it had fallen to 16.68 by Wednesday. From Wednesday to Friday, it climbed about 16.6%. Both statements are true: lower over the full week, sharply higher from the midweek low. Always check the starting point before reacting to a percentage in a headline. VIX describes expected volatility, not a guaranteed direction. No single reading proves a bottom.

Oil was the other major pressure point. Reuters reported Friday settlements of $103.27 for West Texas crude and $116.72 for Brent. Brent finished the week about six percent higher. These are futures prices, not the returns of an oil ETF. Oil funds can behave differently because of contracts, rolling and fees. For the broad market, the concern is what sustained energy costs could do to inflation and company margins.

Reuters linked the supply concern to Iran aligned Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia. Authorities in the United Arab Emirates also reported an oil spill, but its cause was not identified in that report. Don't merge separate incidents into one confirmed story. For Iran, the United States and Israel, watch verified statements, shipping conditions and actual supply disruptions. Escalation could keep pressure on oil; credible de escalation could reduce it. Neither outcome is established in advance.

The transmission is simple, but not automatic. Higher energy costs can squeeze transport and manufacturing margins, affect household spending, and complicate the inflation outlook. Energy producers may benefit from higher selling prices, while other businesses face higher costs. That helps explain why energy can rise while the broad index falls. How long the shock lasts matters more than a single dramatic headline, and companies differ in their hedging, costs and ability to pass prices on.

A separate story worth understanding is PHP2Shell. The Dutch National Cyber Security Centre documented two critical vulnerabilities and emergency fixes published on October 21. Affected web servers can be exposed to remote code execution, meaning an attacker may be able to run code on the server. But a vulnerability is not proof that every website was breached. Exposure, successful intrusion and confirmed data theft are different claims, and they require different evidence.

Cybersecurity shares were a striking contrast. CrowdStrike gained about 13.27% for the week, Palo Alto Networks 11.27% and Cloudflare 9.93%. News coverage discussed the security threat and improving spending expectations. Those are possible contributors, not proof of one single cause. The investing question is whether demand turns into durable revenue and cash flow. A large share price jump alone cannot answer that.

Now to October 26–30. The Fed meets Tuesday and Wednesday, with its decision on Wednesday. The Bureau of Economic Analysis schedules both the advance third quarter GDP report and September personal income and outlays, which includes PCE inflation, for Thursday, October 29, at 8:30 ET. Both reports are on Thursday. They arrive after the Fed decision, so they can change expectations for future policy, not the decision already announced.

For the Fed, the statement and the explanation can matter as much as the rate decision. Listen for how policymakers balance inflation, employment and growth, and how they discuss energy risks. Don't assume that holding rates steady is automatically friendly or hostile to stocks. The reaction depends partly on what investors expected beforehand. October is not a scheduled economic projections meeting, so don't build your plan around receiving a new official dot plot this week.

GDP measures economic activity. The advance estimate can be revised, and the headline growth rate is annualized. PCE asks a different question: what is happening to consumer prices? Core PCE excludes food and energy directly, so an oil spike does not mechanically appear in that month's core number. Indirect cost effects can take time. Read the monthly pace, annual trend, spending details and revisions together. Strong growth with persistent inflation creates a different policy discussion from weaker growth with cooling prices.

The earnings calendar lists Microsoft and Alphabet after Wednesday's close, followed by Amazon after Thursday's close. That puts three major cloud and AI spending stories beside the macro calendar. For Microsoft, watch Azure and margins. For Alphabet, watch search, cloud and capital spending. For Amazon, watch AWS, retail profitability and investment plans. These companies can move major indexes because of their size. Expectations and guidance matter alongside the reported quarter, and scheduled release times can change.

The wider calendar matters too. Coca Cola and SoFi are listed for Tuesday morning, with Visa after the close. Thursday includes Caterpillar and Eli Lilly before the open, and Intel after the close. Exxon and Chevron are listed for Friday morning. Together they offer clues about consumers, industrial activity, healthcare, chips and energy. This is a selected watchlist, not every company reporting. Check each company's investor relations page before relying on an exact release time.

On the chart, QQQ finished at $704.40, near this week's low of $701.16. Its fifty day average is near $725.81, with the twenty day average higher still. Holding the weekly low and eventually reclaiming those averages would improve the recovery case. Losing the low while volatility rises would strengthen the caution case. These are reference levels and conditional scenarios, not targets or instructions to trade.

For SPY, the weekly low is $758.68. Its fifty day average is around $767.81. The constructive case needs more than one green candle: price stabilizing, broader participation staying healthy, and volatility settling. The weaker case is a break of support while the equal weight market also starts losing strength. Next week's earnings, oil and macro news could change either case quickly. A gap can jump straight through a chart level.

Before the next session, write down the event, the expectation and the market's response. Check whether a price is current, whether a percentage is daily or weekly, and whether a headline comes from a source that can be verified. Then compare price, breadth, volatility and oil. You don't have to react to every notification. Waiting for a clearer picture is a valid choice, especially when several large events arrive close together.

This week was not one uniform collapse. It was a split market, with real damage in parts of AI, stronger participation elsewhere, rising oil and a busy week ahead.

Bitcoin fell almost seven percent from yesterday's local high. Does that mean you should sell everything? A price drop alone cannot answer that.

In our October 30 snapshot, Bitcoin fell from around $81,900 to $76,300. It then rebounded toward $78,500. That's a bounce, not a confirmed bottom.

Technology stocks also weakened. QQQ closed down about 1.4% on Thursday. That fits broader risk pressure, but it doesn't prove what caused every Bitcoin sale. Liquidations could amplify a drop; verified totals weren't available.

I couldn't verify a current Binance hack claim in the sources available to me. Treat it as unconfirmed. And a hacked exchange would not automatically mean the Bitcoin network was hacked. Custody risk and price risk are different.

On the hourly chart, watch the overnight low around $76,300. Then watch $79,000 to $80,000. Reclaiming that area, and holding a retest, would be an early improvement. A brief spike isn't enough.

Fresh lows below that overnight floor would keep the downside risk open. Holding above $80,000 would improve the picture, with the prior high near $82,000 still overhead. These are scenarios, not predictions.

Someone holding without leverage for years faces a different decision from someone using borrowed money or needing cash soon. Check your exposure and your custody arrangements. Don't turn an unverified headline into an all or nothing decision.

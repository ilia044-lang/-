# Script template — educational kids episode

The goal is a video a parent chooses on purpose. That means the lesson has to
be visible from the outside — in the title, the thumbnail, and the first ten
seconds — not buried in a story that happens to contain it.

## Before writing: name the rule

Write one sentence a five-year-old could repeat. Not "be careful around
animals" — that's a theme. "Never touch a dog you don't know without asking a
grown-up" — that's a rule.

If the rule can't be said in one sentence, the episode is trying to teach too
much. One rule per episode.

## Two different structures — pick by length

A Short and a four-minute film are not the same object. A Short opens
mid-action because a viewer decides in two seconds. A film needs an opening, a
title, acts and an ending, because a viewer who chose to press play expects to
be taken somewhere. Using Short structure for a film is why a long video can
feel like it "starts strangely" — it begins with no frame around it.

### Structure A — the film (2 minutes and up)

**1. Cold open (0:00–0:12).** A short hook before anything else: the characters
arriving, a question asked, something curious spotted. It sets place and mood
and gives the viewer a reason to stay. It does *not* teach yet.

**2. Title card (0:12–0:18).** The film's name, animated, with music. This is
the single clearest signal that the viewer is watching a made thing rather than
a clip. Generate it as a motion graphic — **never as generated video with text
in it**, because generated on-screen text comes out garbled. Once the
channel's original theme song exists (see `references/theme-song.md`), use the
full ~25-second theme here instead of a plain sting — it is the channel's
biggest piece of brand recognition and should run unchanged on every episode.

**3. Act one — setup.** The world and the characters doing something ordinary,
so the audience knows who everyone is before anything is asked of them.

**4. Act two — the body.** The lessons, the rules, the story beats. This is
where the repeating unit lives.

**5. Act three — payoff.** The consequence. Something is better because of what
the characters chose.

**6. Recap (last 20s).** Quick callbacks to each beat.

**7. Outro card + CTA (last 10s).** Animated end card with the channel name and
the spoken call to action, over continuing motion.

Steps 2 and 7 are motion graphics, not generated video. They cost far less than
a generated clip and they look sharper.

### Structure B — the Short (under 60s)

Open mid-action, no title, no cold open. Hook, situation, pause, rule,
demonstration, repeat, CTA. Details below.

**Beat 1 — Hook (0:00–0:03).** Open mid-motion on something curious or slightly
wrong. A question from the narrator works well: "What is Bubi about to touch?"
Never open on a logo, a title card, or a slow pan. The first two seconds decide
everything on both Shorts and suggested-video traffic.

**Beat 2 — Situation (0:03–0:15).** Bubi encounters the thing. The stakes stay
gentle — curiosity, not danger. Frightening imagery loses parents instantly,
and fear isn't what makes a lesson stick at this age.

**Beat 3 — The pause (0:15–0:25).** Bubi stops and thinks. This is the most
important beat, because it models the behaviour you want: stopping before
acting. Give it real screen time.

**Beat 4 — The rule, spoken (0:25–0:35).** State it plainly, in the narrator's
voice, in the simplest words available. This is the sentence the parent came
for.

**Beat 5 — The right action (0:35–0:50).** Bubi does the correct thing and it
works out. Show the good outcome — children learn the rule from the
consequence, not from the instruction.

**Beat 6 — Repeat the rule (0:50–1:00).** Say it once more, slightly differently.
Repetition is how it lands. This is also the natural thumbnail moment.

**Beat 7 — CTA (1:00–1:10).** Warm, aimed at the parent, delivered over
continuing motion. See the CTA section in SKILL.md.

Total: roughly 60–75 seconds. Long enough to teach, short enough to hold.

## Voiceover writing

- Short sentences. One idea each.
- Concrete words. "Ask a grown-up," not "seek assistance from an adult."
- Leave silence. Wall-to-wall narration is exhausting and hides the animation.
- Address the child directly at the rule ("Remember — never touch...").
- Read it aloud before generating. Anything awkward to say is awkward to hear.

## Shot list

Convert each beat into clips of 15 seconds or less, then write the prompt for
each using `references/prompt-craft.md`. Note which beat becomes the thumbnail
and which becomes the Short — decide both while scripting, not afterwards.

## Worked example — the existing puppy episode

The scheduled "Bubi Helps an Injured Puppy" episode is the model to follow. Its
description already does the right things: it names the safety lesson
explicitly, tells parents what the child will learn, and keeps the tone gentle.
Reuse that pattern.

Lesson: never touch, feed, or medicate an unfamiliar dog without a trusted
adult.

That's one sentence, it's concrete, and a parent can see the value before
pressing play. Every episode should clear that bar.

## Titles and descriptions

Handled by `bubipop-channel-ops` — but write the script knowing the title will
need to carry the lesson. If you can't imagine a title a parent would click,
the episode premise is the problem, not the packaging.

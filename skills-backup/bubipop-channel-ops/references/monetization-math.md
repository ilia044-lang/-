# Monetization math — BubiPop Kids

Read this when Ilia asks about money, RPM, the $2,000/month goal, monetization
thresholds, or "when will this start earning".

Everything here has a shelf life. YouTube changes thresholds and RPMs move.
Verify with a search before quoting figures as current.

## Thresholds (verified September 2026)

**Full monetization (ad revenue + Premium share):**
- 1,000 subscribers, AND
- 4,000 qualified public watch hours in the last 12 months, OR
- 10 million qualified public Shorts views in the last 90 days

The two paths never combine. Shorts-feed watch time does **not** count toward
the 4,000 hours. Private, unlisted, and deleted videos don't count. Artificial
traffic doesn't count.

**Early-access tier (500 subscribers + 3 public uploads in 90 days + 3,000 watch
hours or 3M Shorts views):** unlocks fan funding only — memberships, Super
Thanks, Super Chat, Shopping.

**Why the early tier is irrelevant to this channel:** every one of those
features is disabled on Made for Kids content. Reaching 500 subscribers unlocks
a set of tools the channel cannot use. Do not present it as a revenue milestone.

Reported change ahead: thresholds are said to rise on 1 February 2027 (roughly
8,000 watch hours in 365 days or 20M Shorts views for new applicants). Re-verify
before relying on this — it affects whether speed matters.

## RPM for Made for Kids

Only contextual (non-personalized) ads can serve on kids content, and contextual
ads pay far less.

- General-audience content: roughly $5–15 RPM
- Made for Kids: roughly $1–3 RPM
- Some sources report $0.50–$2, and CPMs of $1–7 depending on niche
- Educational content and toy reviews sit at the higher end, because advertisers
  are reaching parents with purchase intent
- Generic animation and nursery-rhyme content sits at the low end

BubiPop's current content — animated character shorts — is closer to the low end
than the high end. An educational angle ("Bubi Learns Electrical Safety" is
already one) is worth more per view than pure entertainment. That is a real
lever and worth naming when discussing format.

## The $2,000/month calculation

Ads only, kids RPM:

| RPM | monthly views needed for $2,000 |
|---|---|
| $3 | ~667,000 |
| $2 | ~1,000,000 |
| $1 | ~2,000,000 |

**How to use this:** don't just show the table. Compute the multiple against
current views. At the first audit (111 views/28d) the gap was roughly
6,000x–18,000x. Recompute every time — the multiple is the honest framing, and
it makes clear that this is a business-model question, not a tactics question.

Also note: reaching the *threshold* (1,000 subs + 4,000 hours) and reaching the
*goal* ($2,000/month) are separated by a factor of hundreds. Crossing into YPP
is not the finish line; it's the moment revenue becomes nonzero.

## Where kids channels actually earn

Ads are the weakest leg for this content type. The stronger ones:

1. **Brand deals** — toy and edtech companies. Negotiated directly, not subject
   to COPPA's personalized-ad restrictions, so the pricing isn't crippled the
   way AdSense is. Needs an audience worth buying, which is a Gate 2+ problem.
2. **Content licensing** — streaming services and kids platforms license
   existing catalogues. Requires depth: a real library, consistent characters,
   consistent quality.
3. **24/7 pre-recorded live streams** — turns an existing catalogue into ongoing
   view volume. Only works once there *is* a catalogue worth looping.

All three require the same precondition: content people actually watch. That's
why Gate 0 comes first regardless of how appealing the later options sound.

## Compliance — the thing that can zero everything

COPPA misclassification carries FTC penalties (the per-violation civil penalty
was $53,088 in 2025). Correct Made for Kids designation is not optional, and
YouTube now auto-reclassifies under-designated content retroactively, applying
the full restriction set.

Separately, the former "repetitious content" policy is now "inauthentic
content," enforced against AI-generated spam, lazy compilations, and
mass-produced output. Monetization reviews now include stricter human review.

If the channel's output pattern would look mass-produced to a reviewer, raise it
before recommending any increase in cadence. A rejection or demonetization
doesn't slow the plan down — it ends it.

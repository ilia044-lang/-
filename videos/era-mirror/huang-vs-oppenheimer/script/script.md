## S01 · Cold open

`[ON SCREEN: 3D night street 1943, parked car, one lit window (Reconstruction). Smash cut to Huang photo + his six words. Counter 68 YEARS.]`

1. Before you judge Jensen Huang, look at what one night in 1943 cost America.
2. That spring, the man building the atomic bomb spent a night with a woman the government suspected of ties to the enemy.
3. Army agents sat outside her apartment until morning, and wrote it all down.
4. Eleven years later, that night was read back to him in a closed room in Washington. He lost.
5. And it took the United States sixty-eight years to admit the process was rigged against him.
6. Now the man who builds the chips behind American A.I. has admitted an affair with a woman U.S. intelligence believes was recruited by China.
7. On November eighteenth, Congress wants him in a closed room too.
8. So what did America actually find the last time? And why did it take the whole thing back?

## S02 · What is confirmed

`[ON SCREEN: Date stamp NOV 8 2026. Memo card: 'I was unfaithful to my wife.' Two columns build: CONFIRMED / NOT CONFIRMED. Silhouette for Alice Huang, no face.]`

1. Start with what is confirmed.
2. On Sunday, November eighth, 2026, Jensen Huang emailed Nvidia's employees. Six words mattered. I was unfaithful to my wife.
3. The same day, Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation of its own founder.
4. The woman is Alice Huang. No relation. She is a Chinese citizen, and a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore company.
5. According to the New York Times, Megaspeed bought about four point eight billion dollars of Nvidia equipment.
6. Here is what is not confirmed. U.S. intelligence officials believe she was recruited by Beijing.
7. That is an assessment, reported by the Times and by N.B.C. News. Nobody has been charged with anything.
8. China calls the claim baseless. And Nvidia's board says it has no reason to believe that any secrets were compromised.
9. But one sentence is now a problem. In October, before the admission, Huang told Bloomberg the relationship was, quote, completely normal and not unusual.

## S03 · The stake

`[ON SCREEN: Stock line drops, counter to $500 BILLION. Quote card 'Jensen is not replaceable'. Push in on Capitol. Hard cut to black: 'America has asked this before.']`

1. Why does a private affair shake a market?
2. Because one Wall Street analyst says Nvidia would be worth twenty-five to thirty percent less without him.
3. In two days, the stock lost more than ten percent. Almost five hundred billion dollars.
4. In Washington, the question is simpler. Can the man at the center of America's most strategic technology be trusted?
5. America has asked that exact question before. About a different man. And a different technology.

## S04 · The night

`[ON SCREEN: Sepia grade. Oppenheimer portrait in 2.5D parallax. Map flight Berkeley to San Francisco. 3D street again: car, agents, clock running to dawn. Card: 'Phone tap: nothing found.']`

1. Spring, 1943. Robert Oppenheimer is about to leave for Los Alamos, to run the most secret project in American history.
2. Before he goes, he visits Jean Tatlock, in San Francisco. She was his first real love. She was also a member of the Communist Party.
3. He stays the night. He does not know that agents from Army intelligence are waiting outside, watching until morning.
4. Their report goes to the F.B.I.
5. The Bureau taps her phone. It finds nothing.
6. About six months later, Jean Tatlock takes her own life. She was never shown to have passed a single secret.
7. The file stays open anyway.

## S05 · The charges

`[ON SCREEN: Trinity fireball. Timeline slides 1945 to 1953. Borden quote typed on letterhead. Strauss portrait. The real charge letter scan, camera pushes to the Tatlock line, highlighted.]`

1. Ten years pass. The bomb works. The war ends. Oppenheimer becomes the most famous scientist in America.
2. And he starts saying things powerful people do not want to hear. He argues against a crash program to build the hydrogen bomb.
3. In November 1953, a former congressional staff director named William Borden writes to J. Edgar Hoover.
4. His conclusion. More probably than not, Oppenheimer is an agent of the Soviet Union.
5. Four days before Christmas, the chairman of the Atomic Energy Commission, Lewis Strauss, calls him in. His clearance is suspended. He can resign quietly, or fight.
6. He fights. Two days later, the charges arrive. Twenty-four of them.
7. And there, in an official government letter, is the night from 1943. Intimately associated with Doctor Jean Tatlock.

## S06 · Mirror one: the file

`[ON SCREEN: Split screen 1943 | 2026. Globe flight Washington - Anchorage - Beijing. Labels: REPORTED BY NYT / AXIOS. Trump quote card.]`

1. Now hold that next to 2026.
2. According to reporting by the New York Times and Axios, a classified memo about Jensen Huang's relationship circulated inside the Trump administration last spring.
3. It reportedly drew on surveillance of Alice Huang.
4. And in May, on Air Force One, on the way to Beijing, Secretary of State Marco Rubio is reported to have warned the president directly.
5. Huang joined that trip in Alaska. The president says the warning did not make much of an impact on him.
6. In both stories, the file existed long before anyone said a word in public.

## S07 · The rigged room

`[ON SCREEN: Date APRIL 12 1954. 3D hearing room build (Reconstruction): table, three chairs, sofa. Counter 273 wiretap reports. Calendar: 7 days, prosecutor + judges icons together, defense icon outside the door.]`

1. April twelfth, 1954. A temporary government building off Constitution Avenue. An office turned into a courtroom. No press. No public.
2. Oppenheimer believes a fair hearing will clear his name. Here is what he does not know.
3. The F.B.I. has bugged his lawyer's office.
4. The prosecution reads at least two hundred seventy-three wiretap reports, including his conversations with his own attorneys.
5. And for the week before the hearing began, the prosecutor, Roger Robb, sat with the three men who would judge him, going through the F.B.I. files.
6. The defense was not in the room. The defense was never allowed to see those files at all.

## S08 · Cross-examination

`[ON SCREEN: Counter 27 HOURS. Transcript lines type on in Q/A form. 'Because I was an idiot.' fills the frame. Then the Tatlock exchange, one line at a time.]`

1. He testifies for twenty-seven hours. And Robb is ready.
2. He has a secret recording from 1943, of Oppenheimer telling a security officer a story about a spy approach. Parts of that story were invented.
3. Robb reads it back, and asks him why he did it.
4. Oppenheimer's answer is five words. Because I was an idiot.
5. Then comes the night. You spent the night with her, didn't you?
6. Yes.
7. That is when you were working on a secret war project?
8. Yes.

`[ON SCREEN: Comparison split. Left 1954: 'Because I was an idiot.' Right 2026: 'completely normal' strikes through, replaced by 'I was unfaithful'.]`

1. Two men, seventy-two years apart. Each one gave a version of his private life that did not survive the record.

## S11 · The verdict

`[ON SCREEN: Vote tally 2-1 slams in. Real verdict page scan; highlight 'no evidence of disloyalty'. Then 4-1. Calendar: June 29, June 30, July 1 - contract expiry circled.]`

1. May twenty-seventh. The board votes two to one.
2. Read the verdict slowly, because it is one of the strangest in American history.
3. It finds no evidence of disloyalty. It records his love of country. And it recommends that his clearance be taken anyway.
4. On June twenty-ninth, the commission confirms it, four to one.
5. Not for leaking. Not for spying. For what it called fundamental defects in his character.
6. His consulting contract was due to expire two days later. They could have let it run out. They ruled anyway.

## S12 · The turn

`[ON SCREEN: Counter 300 to 70,000 warheads. 1959 memo quote. 1963 Fermi Award photo with LBJ in parallax. DEC 16 2022: the real DOE order scan, highlight the 'discredit' sentence. Title: THE PRIVATE LIFE WAS THE TOOL. THE POLICY WAS THE TARGET.]`

1. So, was America safer?
2. In 1954, the United States had about three hundred nuclear weapons. In the decades that followed, it built some seventy thousand.
3. Historians argue the hearing silenced the strongest voice for restraint.
4. And the case itself did not hold up. In 1959, a lawyer for the commission reviewed it internally. He wrote that the system failed, and that a substantial injustice was done to a loyal American.
5. In 1963, President Johnson handed Oppenheimer the Fermi Award. He died in 1967. The verdict was still on the books.
6. Then, on December sixteenth, 2022, the Secretary of Energy formally vacated it.
7. The order says the process was flawed, and broke the commission's own rules.
8. And it says why it happened. Less out of concern for secrets. More out of a desire to discredit Doctor Oppenheimer in public debates over nuclear weapons policy.
9. That is the lesson of 1954. The private life was the tool. The policy was the target.

## S13 · Mirror three: the policy fight

`[ON SCREEN: 3D chip model rotates. Map: chips flow US to China, gate opens DEC 2025 (H200). Capitol. Text: 'The fight had a new weapon.']`

1. Now look at what surrounds Jensen Huang.
2. For years, Washington has been fighting over one question. How many advanced chips should America sell to China?
3. Huang has argued in public for keeping that market open. Last December, the administration allowed Nvidia's H 200 chips to be sold there.
4. Lawmakers in both parties want to restrict them.
5. Within days of his admission, Senator Elizabeth Warren was urging Congress to pass those limits. The policy fight had a new weapon.

## S14 · The differences

`[ON SCREEN: Clean two-column ledger, rows appear one by one. 1954 column / 2026 column. Last row: 'Evidence tested in court: none / none'.]`

1. None of this makes Huang another Oppenheimer. The differences are real.
2. Oppenheimer held a government clearance. Huang runs a private company.
3. In 1954, investigators had watched for more than a decade, and never claimed that a single secret left his hands.
4. In 2026, nobody outside the government knows yet what, if anything, reached Beijing.
5. Jean Tatlock was never shown to be a spy. About Alice Huang, there is an intelligence assessment that no court has tested.
6. If secrets were passed, that is a security case. And it should be made with evidence.

1. But 1954 leaves a test for the closed room on November eighteenth. Three questions.

3. Two. Does the accused get to see the file?
4. Three. Who wins the policy fight if he falls?
5. America skipped all three with Oppenheimer.
6. It took sixty-eight years, forty-three senators, and every living director of Los Alamos to get the answer on paper.
7. The board that judged him wrote that he was loyal. Watch what the next board writes.
8. The next mirror is on your screen now.
